# Value Proposition & Market Positioning

---

## 1. The Market Problem

The Architecture, Engineering, and Construction sector is undergoing its deepest transformation since CAD was introduced in the 1980s. AI is reshaping what can be designed, how quickly, and at what cost. The gap is not tool adoption — it is practitioner capability.

**The practitioner gap:**
```
Architects                    AI Engineers
- Deep domain knowledge       - ML/AI expertise
- Project delivery experience - Software architecture skills
- Regulatory understanding    - Data pipeline knowledge
- Design process fluency      - Systems thinking
         │                              │
         └──────── Almost no one ───────┘
                   has both.
```

MIAW's value proposition is closing this gap — producing practitioners who operate fluently in both worlds.

## 2. Audience Segments

### Segment 1: Mid-career AEC Professional

**Profile:** 5–15 years practice experience. Architect, structural engineer, construction manager, BIM specialist. Has seen AI tools. Doesn't know how to build with AI. Feels the profession changing around them.

**Pain:** Their practice is changing, they know AI is relevant, but every "AI for architects" course they've tried teaches tools that change every 6 months. They want to understand the underlying logic.

**MIAW's proposition:** A curriculum structured around your design process, not tools. Use your own real project. Produce work you can actually use. Leave with a portfolio and a new capability.

**Channel:** Architecture office leadership. AEC conferences. LinkedIn. Direct referral from industry contacts.

---

### Segment 2: AI/Software Engineer Entering Physical World

**Profile:** 3–10 years software development. Interested in embodied AI, robotics, construction tech, generative design. Has the technical skills but lacks AEC domain knowledge. Finds pure ML research too abstract.

**Pain:** Building AI tools for AEC without domain expertise is technically possible but commercially weak. They don't know the regulatory constraints, the project delivery models, the data formats, or the actual decision-making process on a project.

**MIAW's proposition:** 18 weeks with serious AEC practitioners. Understand the domain deeply enough to build tools that work in real practice. Build a professional network in AEC that most AI engineers lack entirely.

**Channel:** GitHub. Hacker News. AI engineering communities. LinkedIn. Developer AEC tools ecosystem.

---

### Segment 3: Academic Researcher Moving into Practice

**Profile:** PhD or postdoc in computational design, building simulation, urban modeling, or adjacent field. Wants to apply research in real projects but lacks the integration skills to make it work in professional practice.

**Pain:** Research tools rarely survive contact with real projects. The gap between proof-of-concept and deployable workflow is enormous. Universities don't teach integration.

**MIAW's proposition:** F0 through F8 is exactly the integration journey — from individual tool to coherent practice-embedded system. Module F8 specifically builds the student's own adaptive ecosystem.

**Channel:** Academic AEC networks. Computational design communities (Grasshopper forums, AEC Tech). Research lab contacts.

---

### Segment 4: AEC Startup Founder / Product Lead

**Profile:** Building software for the AEC sector. Needs to understand how AI can be integrated into existing workflows. May have technical team but lacks deep AEC domain knowledge or AI architecture experience.

**Pain:** Building for a sector you don't deeply understand leads to products that don't fit professional workflows. The AEC buying process is long; failed products are expensive.

**MIAW's proposition:** The cohort network is itself a value — 30 practitioners who have worked through AI integration problems with their real projects, across different specialties and geographies. The program is a node in the AEC × AI professional network.

## 6. Pricing Rationale

**Range: €4,000–8,000 total**

Comparable AEC professional education:
- BIM certification programs: €1,500–3,000
- Architecture technology conferences: €500–2,000/year
- In-company training days: €2,000–5,000
- Traditional part-time masters: €10,000–25,000/year

At €6,000, MIAW is significantly cheaper than a traditional masters, significantly more structured than certification programs, and designed to be compatible with active professional practice (8–10 hours/week).

**Founder pricing:**
Early waitlist members receive a meaningful discount (target: 20–30% off final price). This rewards early signal and creates urgency around the waitlist conversion without false scarcity.

## 8. North Star Metric

**Qualified applications per cohort ≥ 2× cohort capacity (60 qualified applicants for 30 seats)**

This metric ensures selectivity (brand premium), provides a waitlist for subsequent cohorts, and creates a natural quality signal for the program.

Leading indicators:
- Waitlist conversion rate from `cta_shown` to `waitlist_submitted`
- Visitor type distribution (must include architects + engineers, not only one)
- Conversation turn depth (average turns before drop-off)
- Referral rate (what % of new visitors came from a referral link)
